# OctoAcme Project Management Overview

OctoAcme follows a structured, iterative project management approach centered on clear ownership and customer value. The process is driven by three core personas: Product Managers, who define outcomes and prioritize the backlog; Project Managers, who coordinate delivery, schedules, and risk; and Developers, who build and test features. The project lifecycle moves through five distinct stages: Initiation, Planning, Execution, Release, and Close/Retrospective, ensuring that every initiative is validated with a "Project One-pager" before moving into active development.

The workflow emphasizes small, manageable increments and high visibility. During planning, teams break work into a prioritized backlog with a defined Definition of Done (DoD) and acceptance criteria. Execution is managed via project boards (tracking states from Backlog to Done) and a specific Pull Request workflow that encourages small PRs (ideally ≤ 400 lines) to ensure quality. Quality Assurance is integrated directly into the CI pipeline, requiring automated linting, unit tests, and security scanning, followed by integration and smoke tests before any production release.

Communication is maintained through a disciplined cadence designed to surface blockers early. This includes daily 15-minute standups, weekly delivery syncs, and monthly stakeholder updates. Risks are managed through a formal Risk Register and a three-level escalation path, moving from team-level triage to sponsor-level intervention if necessary. For incidents or releases, OctoAcme uses standardized templates to ensure consistent updates across engineering, sales, and support teams.

Finally, OctoAcme fosters a culture of continuous improvement through mandatory retrospectives held after sprints, releases, or major milestones. These sessions focus on capturing what went well and identifying actionable improvements with clear owners and due dates. By feeding these validated improvements back into the living documentation, the team ensures that their institutional knowledge evolves and remains accessible to all members, reducing dependency risks and accelerating onboarding.
